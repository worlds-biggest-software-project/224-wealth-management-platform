## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **MiFID II (EU)** — investment services directive mandating best-execution reporting, product transparency, client categorisation, and suitability assessments
- **SEC Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)** — US standard requiring advisors to act in clients' best interests; drives suitability and disclosure workflows
- **XBRL / FINRA reporting** — structured financial reporting formats required for regulatory submissions
- **GIPS (Global Investment Performance Standards)** — CFA Institute standards for performance reporting; increasingly expected by institutional clients
- **GDPR / CCPA** — data privacy regulations governing client data held in CRM and financial planning modules
- **FIX Protocol** — standard for electronic trade communication relevant to portfolio trading and order management integrations
- **DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)** — EU regulation effective 2025; requires wealth platform vendors to demonstrate third-party risk controls

## Market Research

**Market Size:** The wealth management platform market reached approximately $7.88 billion in 2026 and is projected to reach $11.82 billion by 2031, at an 11.63% CAGR. Some estimates place the 2026 figure closer to $9.6 billion when broader platform categories are included.

**Funding:** SS&C Technologies and Envestnet are publicly traded. Orion, AdvisorEngine, and Advyzon have received significant private equity backing. The WealthTech sector attracted over $3 billion in funding in 2024–2025.

**Pricing Landscape:** Advisor-facing platforms typically charge $50–$200 per advisor per month for SMB practices, or AUM-based fees (2–5 basis points) for larger books. Enterprise platforms (Salesforce FSC, SS&C) run seven-figure annual contracts. DORA compliance is diverting R&D budgets toward vendor risk management, compressing feature investment.

**Key Buyer Personas:** Registered Investment Advisors (RIAs) seeking all-in-one practice management; enterprise wealth management firms replacing legacy systems; family offices needing consolidated reporting; broker-dealers upgrading advisor productivity tooling.

**Notable Trends:** 64% of wealth management firms have adopted digital advisory tools. Cloud deployment is the fastest-growing segment. Onboarding and KYC tools growing at 14.63% CAGR due to perpetual client due diligence requirements. Neobank-embedded wealth modules are compressing pricing for standalone advisors. Around 59% of firms are deploying cloud platforms.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- AI-driven suitability and financial planning: LLMs could generate personalised financial plans from client questionnaire data, simulate retirement scenarios, and flag suitability concerns relative to Reg BI or MiFID II requirements automatically.
- Portfolio rebalancing and tax optimisation: ML-based rebalancing engines could simultaneously minimise tax drag, maintain target allocations, and respect client-specific ESG exclusions across thousands of accounts.
- Intelligent client engagement scoring: AI models could analyse client behaviour signals (portal logins, document views, life events) to predict dissatisfaction and trigger proactive advisor outreach before churn occurs.
- Automated regulatory reporting: Generative AI could draft GIPS-compliant performance commentaries, client suitability reports, and regulatory disclosures using structured portfolio data, reducing advisor administrative time.
- Natural-language client portal: Conversational AI interfaces could allow clients to ask questions about their portfolio, run "what-if" scenario analyses, and request transactions using plain language, improving digital engagement rates.
